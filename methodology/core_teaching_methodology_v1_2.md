## 2. Epistemic Framework & Explicit Limitations

### 2.1 Three-Tier Governance Classification
To prevent treating provisional product numbers as scientific laws—or treating core learning principles as optional styling—every rule in Version 1.2 carries one of three explicit classifications:

| Classification Tag | Definition | Examples in Version 1.2 | Governance Rule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`[Evidence-Informed Principle]`** | Instructional design principles grounded in cognitive psychology and learning sciences, recognizing that real-world effectiveness depends on learner age, domain, and context. | Working memory constraints (Cognitive Load Theory), pairing visuals with concise text (Dual Coding), fading worked examples for novices, active retrieval practice over passive re-reading, and varying problem formats. | **Guides pedagogical design.** Preserved across all lessons, with implementation adapted to the subject and grade band. |
| **`[Mandatory V1 Product Constraint]`** | Fixed product-scope, safety, and architectural boundaries defining Version 1 of this product. | Landscape A4 print format, graphite pencil only, zero external tools or screens, 2 student pages + 1 answer key page, stateless operation, source-grounded claims, and non-guarantee framing. | **Strict V1 engineering requirement.** Any violation blocks lesson delivery. |
| **`[Provisional V1 Heuristic — Requires Testing]`** | Practical engineering estimates, numerical thresholds, timing weights, word caps, and layout dimensions chosen as buildable starting points for Version 1. | The $780\text{–}990\text{ sec}$ ($13.0\text{–}16.5\text{ min}$) corridor, grade-band WPM reading/writing speeds, passive reading word caps ($45/80/120\text{ words}$), handwriting caps ($15/40/65/80\text{ words}$), and $\le 70\%$ content box area. | **Tunable configuration parameters.** Enforced in software for V1, but must be empirically calibrated through observational testing with real children. |

### 2.2 Explicit Non-Guarantee Contract (`[Mandatory V1 Product Constraint]`)
1. **No Guarantee of Mastery:** A single 15-minute worksheet cannot guarantee permanent retention or mastery, which require distributed practice, feedback, and consolidation over time. The objective of a Version 1.2 lesson is **Initial Comprehension and Guided First Application**: helping a child encounter new curriculum material for the first time, grasp its core mechanism, and apply it with fading support.
2. **No Guarantee of Engagement:** Real-world children experience fluctuating attention, fatigue, and frustration. This methodology treats **attention as a design constraint** (minimizing friction, confusion, and monotony) and **learning as the primary objective**, never claiming that any worksheet format guarantees excitement or focus.
3. **No Automated Guarantee of Educational Quality:** Passing all deterministic software checks (`DET`) and AI-assisted checks (`AI`) filters out structural, factual, and layout defects, but **does not guarantee educational quality or child comprehension**. Ongoing human review and observational child testing (`HUM`) remain essential.

### 2.3 Primary Input Key: Grade-Band Selection & Fallback Protocol (`[Mandatory V1 Product Constraint]`)
To eliminate ambiguous or overlapping age ranges, all rules, timing weights, and ergonomic specifications in Version 1.2 are indexed by **four non-overlapping Grade Bands** (`G1_2`, `G3_5`, `G6_8`, `G9_PLUS`). Typical approximate ages are listed for reference only:

| Grade Band Key | Target School Grades | Reference Age Range (Non-Overlapping) | Primary Developmental Profile |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`G1_2`** | **Grades 1–2** | Ages 6–7 | Emergent/early readers; developing fine-motor control; requires large ruled lines, minimal writing, and heavy visual scaffolding. |
| **`G3_5`** | **Grades 3–5** | Ages 8–10 | Developing instructional reading fluency; capable of short-phrase writing, diagram labelling, and guided multi-step procedures. |
| **`G6_8`** | **Grades 6–8** | Ages 11–13 | Fluent readers; capable of abstract rules, multi-step symbolic procedures, error analysis, and 1–2 sentence causal explanations. |
| **`G9_PLUS`** | **Grades 9+** | Ages 14+ | Advanced readers; capable of formal notation, multi-variable problem solving, and concise analytical synthesis. |

#### Authoritative Grade-Band Resolution & Clarification Rule
The software resolves the target `grade_band` using this strict three-step precedence order:
1. **Parent-Selected Grade (Primary Authority & Reliable Fallback):** Whenever the parent selects or specifies a school grade (or grade band) at upload time, the software uses the parent-selected grade as the authoritative input and maps it deterministically to `G1_2`, `G3_5`, `G6_8`, or `G9_PLUS`. When the uploaded PDF lacks explicit grade markings, the parent-selected grade serves as the reliable fallback.
2. **Explicit Source Curriculum Metadata (Secondary):** If the parent does not provide a grade at upload, the ingestion pipeline checks whether the uploaded PDF explicitly states a single unambiguous grade or year level in its headers/text (e.g., *"Grade 4 Science"*, *"Year 8 Mathematics"*).
3. **No Silent Guessing — Mandatory Clarification Request:** If the parent has not selected a grade **and** the uploaded source PDF does not contain explicit, unambiguous evidence supporting a single grade-band decision (e.g., an unlabeled worksheet scan or a document spanning multiple grade bands), the software **must not guess**. Generation halts prior to lesson creation and prompts the parent to specify the child's target grade band.

## 3. Core Teaching Rules & Real-World Learner Robustness

Real-world children vary widely in reading ability, prior knowledge, attention span, and confidence. Because Version 1.2 operates without student profiles, every generated worksheet embeds universal design buffers directly on the page.

### Rule 1: Source Grounding, Multimodal Extraction, and Traceability
* **Classification:** `[Mandatory V1 Product Constraint]`
* **Requirement:**
  1. **Multimodal Source Ingestion (Supporting Diagram-Heavy PDFs):** The ingestion pipeline extracts both text blocks and visual/diagram content (via OCR and vision-language descriptions) from the uploaded curriculum PDF, indexing each extracted paragraph, equation, table, or diagram as a numbered **Source Unit (`SRC-P{page}-U{index}`)**. A diagram-heavy or math-heavy PDF with fewer than 50 words of prose is **valid and accepted** provided multimodal extraction identifies at least one coherent curriculum concept, visual model, or worked example. Generation halts for source insufficiency *only* when both text and visual extraction fail to recover a usable instructional topic (e.g., blank, corrupted, or unreadable scans).
  2. **Explicit Separation of Source Claims vs. Pedagogical Scaffolding:**
     * **Source-Grounded Claims & Terminology:** Every domain fact, definition, formula, historical date, classification, and technical term taught in the lesson must be stored in the lesson JSON with a `source_unit_ids` array pointing to the specific `SRC-...` unit(s) that support it. The system must never invent unsupported domain facts or silently introduce external terminology.
     * **Generated Pedagogical Scaffolding:** The system may generate original instructional framing—including curiosity hooks, step-by-step worked breakdowns, new numerical values/scenarios for practice problems (following the exact rule in the cited `SRC-...` unit), and everyday analogies—provided that any analogy is explicitly marked (`is_analogy: true`, phrased as *"Think of it like..."*) and is **never tested as a literal curriculum fact** in `Apply` or `Recall`.
  3. **Transparent Handling of Incomplete Curriculum PDFs:** If an uploaded PDF references a sub-topic without providing enough information to teach it (e.g., listing a review question whose explanation was on an un-uploaded page), the system must restrict the lesson to the concepts that *are* supported in the PDF and state the covered scope clearly on Page 1 and Page 3.
* **Why It Matters:** Parents trust the system to teach the school's actual curriculum accurately, including visual/diagrammatic handouts, without hallucinating outside facts.
* **How Enforced in V1.2:** Deterministic check **`DET-02`** (verifies valid `source_unit_ids` references and multimodal topic presence) + AI-assisted check **`AI-01`** (verifies factual entailment against cited `SRC` units, checks that practice items follow the source method, and confirms analogies are not tested as facts).

### Rule 2: First-Time Learning and Prerequisite Completeness
* **Classification:** `[Evidence-Informed Principle]` + `[Mandatory V1 Product Constraint]`
* **Requirement:** Assume the child is learning the target material for the first time:
  1. **Zero Unexplained Domain Terms:** Every technical term, symbol, or unit used in `Reinforce`, `Vary`, `Apply`, or `Recall` must either be explicitly defined and illustrated in `Discover` or belong to foundational knowledge at least one grade band below the target grade.
  2. **Secondary Skill Buffer:** When teaching a new concept (e.g., a physics relationship or multi-step method), keep secondary arithmetic and general reading vocabulary at or below `Target_Grade - 1` complexity so peripheral calculation or decoding struggles do not block concept learning.
  3. **Pronunciation Guides:** Newly introduced domain terms of $\ge 4$ syllables (or non-phonetic roots) must include a simple inline pronunciation guide on first mention (e.g., **metamorphosis** *[met-uh-MOR-fuh-sis]*).
* **Why It Matters:** Skipping intermediate definitions or using difficult secondary numbers causes novices to stall and guess.
* **How Enforced in V1.2:** AI-assisted check **`AI-02`** (audits `Reinforce`, `Vary`, `Apply`, and `Recall` against `Discover` definitions and checks secondary vocabulary/arithmetic complexity).

### Rule 3: Low-Friction Instructions and Uniform `worked_start` (`Ex:`) Modelling
* **Classification:** `[Evidence-Informed Principle]` (Extraneous Load Reduction) + `[Provisional V1 Heuristic — Requires Testing]` (Word caps)
* **Requirement:** Make every activity effortless to understand and start without adult explanation:
  1. **Direct Imperative Syntax:** Write instructions using concrete *Action–Object–Location* phrasing (e.g., *"Draw a line from each planet in Column A to its description in Column B"*).
  2. **Instruction Sentence Caps:** Each instruction sentence must contain at most **2 sequential action verbs** and stay within grade-band word limits:
     * **`G1_2`:** $\le 12\text{ words}$ per sentence.
     * **`G3_5`:** $\le 18\text{ words}$ per sentence.
     * **`G6_8` & `G9_PLUS`:** $\le 22\text{ words}$ per sentence.
  3. **Authoritative Definition & Rules for `worked_start` (`Ex:`):**
     * A `worked_start` is a **dedicated, ungraded demonstration element (`Item 0 / Ex:`)** rendered in light grey simulated pencil script (`Done for you`) that visually models the required pencil action before the child begins the scored items.
     * Because `worked_start` is an ungraded visual example, it is **never counted** toward the 7–15 scored student items, never subtracted from required distractors, and never generated by stealing a scored student question.
     * **Applicability Across Activity Mechanics:**
       * **Multi-Item Structured Mechanics (`M2_ASYMMETRIC_MATCH`, `M3_DIAGRAM_LABEL`, `M4_CATEGORY_SORT`, `M7_RULE_PATH`):** Must always include an explicit `worked_start` object showing 1 pre-drawn connection line, 1 pre-filled diagram callout, 1 pre-sorted item, or 1 pre-traced starting step.
       * **Step-Completion & Error-Analysis Mechanics (`M5_FADED_STEP`, `M6_SPOT_MISTAKE`):** Must include a `worked_start` consisting of either a pre-completed `Ex:` item or **explicit Step-1 visual modelling** (in `M5`, Step 1 is fully worked and labeled `Step 1 [Done for you]`; in `M6`, the first valid line or an example annotation is marked in grey pencil script to show how to circle and correct the error).
       * **Direct Selection & Open Framing Mechanics (`M1_ANNOTATION`, `M8_MODEL_SKETCH`, `M9_SHORT_SYNTHESIS`):** Do not require a separate `Ex:` problem; instead, `M1` bolds the action verb (`**Circle**`), `M8` provides a pre-drawn reference frame/starter element, and `M9` provides a sentence starter (`G1_2` and `G3_5`) or a target keyword checklist (`Use: [ ] term1 [ ] term2`).
* **Why It Matters:** Children frequently freeze at a new worksheet table or grid because they are unsure what physical mark is expected. A consistent visual `Ex:` removes startup confusion.
* **How Enforced in V1.2:** Deterministic check **`DET-03`** (validates instruction sentence word counts and checks that every `M2–M7` block contains a non-null `worked_start` object separate from the scored `items` array) + AI-assisted check **`AI-03`** (verifies instruction clarity and `worked_start` alignment).

### Rule 4: Active Pencil Participation and Manageable Reading Spans
* **Classification:** `[Evidence-Informed Principle]` (Active Encoding) + `[Provisional V1 Heuristic — Requires Testing]` (Word caps)
* **Requirement:**
  1. **Immediate First Pencil Action:** The opening curiosity hook in `Discover` must lead to a simple, high-confidence observation mark (e.g., circling a feature on a contrasting pair or worked example) after no more than **$35\text{ words}$ (`G1_2`)**, **$50\text{ words}$ (`G3_5`)**, or **$65\text{ words}$ (`G6_8` / `G9_PLUS`)** of introductory text (corresponding to $\le 60\text{ seconds}$ of reading).
  2. **Authoritative Passive Reading Span Ceiling:** No continuous block of instructional prose anywhere in the lesson may exceed the following word ceilings without an interleaved pencil action:
     * **`G1_2`:** Maximum **$45\text{ words}$**.
     * **`G3_5`:** Maximum **$80\text{ words}$**.
     * **`G6_8` & `G9_PLUS`:** Maximum **$120\text{ words}$**.
* **Why It Matters:** Breaking explanations into short blocks followed immediately by a pencil action prevents passive eye-scanning and maintains attention.
* **How Enforced in V1.2:** Deterministic check **`DET-04`** (counts words in the opening hook before `item[0]` and in every continuous prose block between student actions).

### Rule 5: Robustness Against Confusion, Boredom, Frustration, Careless Mistakes, and Shame
* **Classification:** `[Evidence-Informed Principle]` + `[Mandatory V1 Product Constraint]`
* **Requirement:**
  1. **Visual Dual-Coding (Anti-Confusion):** In `Discover`, pair concise text with an annotated diagram, sequence strip, or visual comparison table where callout labels ($\le 15\text{ words}$ per label) sit directly adjacent to the visual feature rather than in a separate numbered legend.
  2. **Mechanic Variation Without Objective Drift (Anti-Boredom):** Use at least **3 distinct paper mechanics** across the lesson, with no two consecutive activity blocks sharing the same mechanic, while keeping all blocks anchored to the same learning objective.
  3. **Low-Floor Entry & Inline Hint Pointers (Anti-Frustration):** Ensure `Discover` and `Reinforce` Item 1 offer high first-try success, and include a brief, non-stigmatizing reference pointer in both the `Vary` and `Apply` blocks on Page 2 (e.g., *"Hint: Look back at Page 1 Step 1"*) so a stuck child knows where to look without calling a parent.
  4. **Careless-Mistake Guards:**
     * **No Negative-Stem Trick Questions:** Do not ask questions where the child must select a *false* or *excluded* statement (e.g., *"Which of the following is NOT true?"* or *"All of these EXCEPT..."*). Natural negation within explanations or domain facts (*"Sponges do not have backbones"*) is permitted.
     * **Pre-Printed Units & Structured Workspaces:** Always pre-print measurement units inside numerical answer blanks (`___ cm`) and provide structured step boxes for multi-step calculations.
  5. **Psychological Safety (Zero Shame, Timers, or Manipulative Rewards):**
     * Never display countdown timers, speed challenges (*"Finish in 15 minutes!"*), point/score boxes (`Score: ___/10`), manipulative extrinsic rewards, or condescending difficulty labels (*"This is easy!"*).
     * Frame error-spotting tasks (`M6_SPOT_MISTAKE`) around a fictional third-person peer (*"Sam tried solving this problem, but made one mix-up..."*) so the child critiques an external error safely.
* **Why It Matters:** Protecting confidence and eliminating trick questions keeps real-world children engaged through challenging steps.
* **How Enforced in V1.2:** Deterministic check **`DET-05`** (enforces mechanic diversity, pre-printed unit fields, and blocks UI score/timer patterns) + Contextual AI checks **`AI-03`** and **`AI-04`** (detects negative-stem trick questions, verifies supportive tone, and checks third-person error framing and hint pointers in `Vary` and `Apply` without falsely blocking domain terms like *"simple machines"* or *"boiling point"*).

### Rule 6: Genuine Understanding Over Mechanical Copying
* **Classification:** `[Evidence-Informed Principle]` (Retrieval Practice & Transfer) + `[Mandatory V1 Product Constraint]`
* **Requirement:**
  1. **Ban on Non-Conceptual Busywork:** Word searches, anagrams, letter-substitution ciphers, unlabelled mazes, and decorative coloring tasks are prohibited.
  2. **Asymmetric Matching & Word Banks:** Every matching task (`M2`) and word-bank task (`M3`) must include **at least 1 extra plausible distractor option** among the student-solved options ($|\text{Student Options}| \ge |\text{Student Prompts}| + 1$) so the last item cannot be solved by elimination.
  3. **Contextual Anti-Copying & Page-2 Recall Isolation:** Questions in `Reinforce`, `Vary`, `Apply`, and `Recall` must paraphrase `Discover` explanations rather than using verbatim fill-in-the-blank sentences. Furthermore, all `Discover` explanations are placed on **Student Page 1**, while `Recall` is placed in the bottom-right isolated box on **Student Page 2** with a top banner instructing single-sided users: *"Flip Page 1 face-down before starting the Final Memory Check!"*
* **Why It Matters:** Prevents students from completing the worksheet by copying visible sentence patterns or guessing the last remaining word-bank choice.
* **How Enforced in V1.2:** Deterministic check **`DET-06`** (enforces approved mechanic enums, verifies $|\text{Student Options}| \ge |\text{Student Prompts}| + 1$ excluding `worked_start`, and verifies Page 1 vs. Page 2 stage placement) + AI-assisted check **`AI-05`** (evaluates prompts contextually to ensure they require comprehension rather than verbatim copying, and checks that sub-items vary meaningful parameters).

### Rule 7: Print-First Autonomy and Landscape A4 Ergonomics
* **Classification:** `[Mandatory V1 Product Constraint]` + `[Provisional V1 Heuristic — Requires Testing]` (Dimensions)
* **Requirement:**
  1. **Pencil-and-Paper Only:** Student activities must require only the 2 printed Landscape A4 pages ($297\text{ mm} \times 210\text{ mm}$) and a standard graphite pencil—no scissors, glue, external rulers, colored pencils, dice, or screens.
  2. **Monochrome-Safe Visuals:** All diagrams and visual elements must remain clear when printed in black-and-white/greyscale, using distinct patterns (stripes, dots, cross-hatching), line weights, and direct text labels rather than relying on color hue to distinguish features.
  3. **Grade-Appropriate Handwriting & Layout Sizing:**
     * **`G1_2`:** Minimum **$12\text{ mm}$** write-in line height (with dashed midline); $\ge 14\text{ pt}$ body font.
     * **`G3_5`:** Minimum **$9\text{ mm}$** write-in line height; $\ge 12\text{ pt}$ body font.
     * **`G6_8` & `G9_PLUS`:** Minimum **$7.5\text{ mm}$** write-in line height; $\ge 10.5\text{ pt}$ body font.
     * **Visual Breathing Room:** Content containers (cards, text boxes, tables, diagrams) must occupy **$\le 70\%$ of the page canvas area** (leaving $\ge 30\%$ open margins, gutters, and padding) with zero text clipping or page overflow.
* **Why It Matters:** Ensures legibility, comfortable handwriting space, and reliable printing on any home monochrome printer.
* **How Enforced in V1.2:** Deterministic check **`DET-07`** (verifies CSS typography/line heights, 2 student pages + 1 answer key page at $297\text{ mm} \times 210\text{ mm}$, zero DOM box-model overflow, and $\le 70\%$ content box area) + Contextual AI check **`AI-03`** (ensures instructions never ask the student to use external tools or identify items by color alone, without falsely blocking domain terms like *"red blood cells"*).

### Rule 8: Answerability, Role-Separated Blind-Solver Evaluation, and Page 3 Parent Answer Key
* **Classification:** `[Mandatory V1 Product Constraint]`
* **Requirement:**
  1. **Clear Intended Answers & Page 3 Parent Answer Key:** Every scored student item on Pages 1–2 must have a clear, unambiguous target answer (or explicit evaluation criteria for synthesis/sketch items) supported by the lesson content. **Page 3 (Parent Answer Key & Scope Guide)** is generated as a separate page containing:
     * The canonical answer for every scored item.
     * Acceptable alternative phrasings for short-answer items.
     * A 1-sentence plain-language explanation of *why* the answer is correct.
     * A transparent Scope & Non-Guarantee Note stating which slice of the uploaded curriculum was covered and reminding the parent that a single 15-minute lesson builds initial comprehension rather than permanent mastery.
  2. **Role-Separated Two-Step Answerability Evaluation (`AI-06`):** To verify answerability without information leakage, automated evaluation strictly separates the **Blind Solver** from the **Answer-Key & Grounding Evaluator**:
     * **Step A — Isolated Blind Solver (Receives *Only* Student Pages 1–2):** Receives only the student-facing content of Pages 1–2 (with zero access to Page 3 or the source curriculum PDF), attempts every scored item, and flags any item that is ambiguous, underspecified, or unanswerable from Pages 1–2 alone.
     * **Step B — Answer-Key & Grounding Evaluator (Compares Solver Output, Page 3 Key, and Source Units):** A separate evaluation step receives the Blind Solver's responses/flags alongside Page 3 (Parent Answer Key) and the cited `SRC` source units, verifying that (i) the Blind Solver raised zero ambiguity flags, (ii) the Blind Solver's answers match the Page 3 `canonical_answer` or `acceptable_variants`, and (iii) the Page 3 answers and explanations accurately reflect the lesson and source material.
* **Why It Matters:** Keeps the blind-solving test genuinely blind to the answer key while ensuring a separate evaluator verifies alignment across the student pages, the parent answer key, and the curriculum source.
* **How Enforced in V1.2:** Deterministic check **`DET-08`** (verifies 1-to-1 item ID coverage and required rubric fields on Page 3) + Two-step AI-assisted check **`AI-06`** (Isolated Blind Solver pass followed by the Answer-Key & Grounding Evaluator pass).

## 4. Reusable Lesson Architecture, Sequence Variants & Page Layout

### 4.1 Functional Stage Specifications & Spatial Layout Rules
Every Version 1.2 lesson includes all five functional stage types (**`Discover`, `Reinforce`, `Vary`, `Apply`, `Recall`**) distributed across a fixed **2-Page Landscape A4 Student Worksheet** (plus Page 3 Parent Answer Key).

To prevent any conflict between **instructional sequence** (the order in which the child completes blocks, numbered sequentially in reading order on the page) and **visual zone placement**, Version 1.2 defines stage rules by **functional stage type** rather than assuming `Vary` is always Step 3:

| Functional Stage Type | Assigned Page & Spatial Zone | Pedagogical Purpose | Support Level | Scored Sub-Items (`[Provisional V1 Heuristic]`) | Target Stage Duration (`[Provisional V1 Heuristic]`) | Max Block Duration Cap |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Header & Setup** | **Page 1**, Top Banner | State lesson title, scope banner (*"Part 1 of N"*), and orient the child. | — | 0 scored items | **$60\text{ sec}$ ($1.0\text{ min}$)** (Fixed overhead) | — |
| **`Discover`** | **Page 1**, Left Zone *(or Micro-Cycles `1A`/`1B` in `V4`)* | Spark curiosity with a short hook, present a dual-coded visual + verbal explanation, and walk through a complete worked example with a guided observation mark. | **Maximum:** Complete worked model provided; child marks/notices a key feature. | **1–2** guided observation items | **$180\text{ sec}$ ($3.0\text{ min}$)** | **$\le 210\text{ sec}$ ($\le 3.5\text{ min}$)** |
| **`Reinforce`** | **Page 1**, Right Zone *(or Micro-Cycles `2A`/`2B` in `V4`)* | Consolidate basic comprehension of the definition, rule, or steps using high-support scaffolding and a grey `Ex:` Worked Start. | **High:** Word bank (with $\ge 1$ distractor), faded final step, or asymmetric matching. | **2–4** scaffolded items *(or $1\text{–}2$ per micro-cycle in `V4`)* | **$180\text{ sec}$ ($3.0\text{ min}$)** | **$\le 210\text{ sec}$ ($\le 3.5\text{ min}$)** |
| **`Vary`** | **Page 2**, Top-Left Zone in `V1, V3, V4` \| **Page 2**, Top-Right/Center Zone in `V2_PROCEDURAL` | Practise the same concept in a shifted format, reverse direction, or third-person *Spot-the-Mistake* activity. | **Moderate:** Format shifted; includes inline reference pointer (*"Check Page 1 Step 1"*). | **2–3** varied items | **$150\text{ sec}$ ($2.5\text{ min}$)** | **$\le 210\text{ sec}$ ($\le 3.5\text{ min}$)** |
| **`Apply`** | **Page 2**, Top-Right/Center Zone in `V1, V3, V4` \| **Page 2**, Top-Left Zone in `V2_PROCEDURAL` | Use the knowledge to solve a realistic problem or explain a novel scenario in a structured workspace. | **Low:** Workspace grid, pre-printed units, or sentence stem provided; no word bank; includes inline reference pointer. | **1–2** application problems | **$210\text{ sec}$ ($3.5\text{ min}$)** | **$\le 270\text{ sec}$ ($\le 4.5\text{ min}$)** |
| **`Recall`** | **Page 2**, Bottom-Right Isolated Box *(Always Final Block)* | Retrieve the core concept or rule from memory without copying from Page 1. Includes banner: *"Flip Page 1 face-down before the Final Memory Check!"* | **Zero Content Hints:** Isolated on Page 2; cue-free retrieval or *"Teach It Back"* summary. | **1–2** retrieval prompts | **$120\text{ sec}$ ($2.0\text{ min}$)** | **$\le 210\text{ sec}$ ($\le 3.5\text{ min}$)** |
| **TOTAL** | **Pages 1–2** *(+ Page 3 Key)* | **Initial Encoding $\rightarrow$ Guided Practice $\rightarrow$ Transfer & Retrieval** | **Progressive Fade to Cue-Free Recall** | **7–15 total scored items** | **$900\text{ sec}$ ($15.0\text{ min}$) Target** | **$780\text{–}990\text{ sec}$ ($13.0\text{–}16.5\text{ min}$) Corridor** |

*Optional Fast-Finisher Buffer:* Page 2 may include one ungraded footer box labeled **`[Optional Bonus Thought]`** with a 1-sentence *"What if?"* reflection question. It is excluded from the 7–15 scored item count and the 15-minute timing calculation.

### 4.2 Authoritative Stage Sequence Variants & Spatial Zone Mapping
To ensure instructional sequence and visual page placement never contradict each other, the layout engine places blocks into spatial zones strictly according to the active variant's reading order (Page 1: Left $\rightarrow$ Right; Page 2: Top-Left $\rightarrow$ Top-Right/Center $\rightarrow$ Bottom-Right Isolated Box):

## 6. Simplified 15-Minute Timing Model & Safe Adjustment Rules

### 6.1 Explicit Unit-Consistent Timing Calculation (`[Provisional V1 Heuristic — Requires Testing]`)
To eliminate unit mismatches between words-per-minute ($\text{WPM}$) rates and second-based ($\text{sec}$) item weights, **all timing components are computed strictly in seconds ($\text{sec}$)** using an explicit $\times 60\text{ sec/min}$ conversion factor before being summed into total lesson duration:

$$T_{\text{block}}(k)\text{ [sec]} = T_{\text{read}}(k)\text{ [sec]} + T_{\text{visual}}(k)\text{ [sec]} + T_{\text{items}}(k)\text{ [sec]} + T_{\text{write}}(k)\text{ [sec]}$$

$$T_{\text{stages}}\text{ [sec]} = \sum_{k=1}^{K} T_{\text{block}}(k)\text{ [sec]}$$

$$T_{\text{lesson}}\text{ [sec]} = T_{\text{orientation}}\text{ [sec]} + T_{\text{stages}}\text{ [sec]} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad T_{\text{lesson}}\text{ [min]} = \frac{T_{\text{lesson}}\text{ [sec]}}{60\text{ sec/min}}$$

Where $K \in \{5, 7\}$ is the number of activity blocks, and each component in seconds ($\text{sec}$) is defined as:
1. **$T_{\text{orientation}}\text{ [sec]}$ (Fixed Setup & Transition Overhead):** **$60\text{ sec}$** ($1.0\text{ min}$) per lesson across all grade bands.
2. **$T_{\text{read}}(k)\text{ [sec]}$ (Block Reading Time in Seconds):**
   $$T_{\text{read}}(k)\text{ [sec]} = \left( \frac{\text{Total Student-Facing Printed Words in Block } k\text{ [words]}}{\text{WPM}_{\text{read}}(\text{grade\_band})\text{ [words/min]}} \right) \times 60\text{ sec/min}$$
3. **$T_{\text{visual}}(k)\text{ [sec]}$ (Diagram & Worked-Model Inspection Time in Seconds):**
   $$T_{\text{visual}}(k)\text{ [sec]} = N_{\text{visuals}}(k) \times 30\text{ sec}$$
   *(where $N_{\text{visuals}}(k)$ is the count of instructional diagrams, data tables, or multi-step worked models in Block $k$).*
4. **$T_{\text{items}}(k)\text{ [sec]}$ (Combined Cognitive Deliberation + Discrete Pencil Action Time in Seconds):**
   $$T_{\text{items}}(k)\text{ [sec]} = \left( N_{\text{scored\_items}}(k) \times w_{\text{stage}}\text{ [sec/item]} \right) + \left( N_{\text{sketch\_items}}(k) \times 20\text{ sec/sketch} \right)$$
   Where $w_{\text{stage}}\text{ [sec/item]}$ is determined by the block's functional stage type:
   * **`Discover` item** (guided observation mark): **$15\text{ sec/item}$**.
   * **`Reinforce` item** (scaffolded match/sort/label/step): **$25\text{ sec/item}$**.
   * **`Vary` item** (shifted format / spot-the-mistake): **$35\text{ sec/item}$**.
   * **`Apply` item** (independent problem / scenario): **$55\text{ sec/item}$** (plus $+20\text{ sec}$ if `mechanic_type == M8_MODEL_SKETCH`).
   * **`Recall` item** (cue-free retrieval check): **$45\text{ sec/item}$** (plus $+20\text{ sec}$ if `mechanic_type == M8_MODEL_SKETCH`).
   *(Note: Ungraded `worked_start` examples and the optional bonus footer are excluded from $N_{\text{scored\_items}}(k)$).*
5. **$T_{\text{write}}(k)\text{ [sec]}$ (Handwriting Execution Time in Seconds):**
   $$T_{\text{write}}(k)\text{ [sec]} = \left( \frac{\text{Expected Student Written Words in Block } k\text{ [words]}}{\text{WPM}_{\text{write}}(\text{grade\_band})\text{ [words/min]}} \right) \times 60\text{ sec/min}$$
   *(where a multi-digit number or symbolic equation step counts as $1\text{ written word}$).*

### 6.2 Timing Corridor, Stage Balance Constraints & Worked Verification (`[Provisional V1 Heuristic — Requires Testing]`)
Every generated lesson must satisfy all four timing constraints simultaneously:
1. **Total Duration Corridor:**
   $$780\text{ sec} \le T_{\text{lesson}}\text{ [sec]} \le 990\text{ sec} \quad \left(13.0\text{ min} \le T_{\text{lesson}}\text{ [min]} \le 16.5\text{ min}; \text{ Target: } 900\text{ sec} = 15.0\text{ min}\right)$$
2. **Per-Block Duration Cap (By Functional Stage Type):**
   * Each `Discover`, `Reinforce`, `Vary`, and `Recall` block must satisfy **$T_{\text{block}}(k) \le 210\text{ sec}$ ($\le 3.5\text{ min}$)**.
   * The `Apply` block must satisfy **$T_{\text{block}}(\text{Apply}) \le 270\text{ sec}$ ($\le 4.5\text{ min}$)** (whether it appears as Step 3 in `V2_PROCEDURAL` or Step 4 in `V1, V3, V4`).
3. **Higher-Order Cognitive Share (`Apply + Recall`):** The combined duration of the `Apply` block and `Recall` block must represent **$35\%$ to $45\%$ of Active Stage Time ($T_{\text{stages}}$)**:
   $$0.35 \le \frac{T_{\text{block}}(\text{Apply})\text{ [sec]} + T_{\text{block}}(\text{Recall})\text{ [sec]}}{T_{\text{stages}}\text{ [sec]}} \le 0.45$$
4. **Handwriting Motor Time Share:** Total handwriting time across the lesson must satisfy:
   $$\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{K} T_{\text{write}}(k)\text{ [sec]}}{T_{\text{stages}}\text{ [sec]}} \le 0.30$$

#### Worked Unit-Consistent Verification Example (`G3_5`: $\text{WPM}_{\text{read}} = 70$, $\text{WPM}_{\text{write}} = 11$)
* **Orientation:** $T_{\text{orientation}} = 60.0\text{ sec}$ ($1.00\text{ min}$).
* **`Discover` Block:** $105\text{ printed words} \implies T_{\text{read}} = (105 / 70) \times 60 = 90.0\text{ sec}$; $2\text{ visuals} \implies T_{\text{visual}} = 60.0\text{ sec}$; $1\text{ item} \implies T_{\text{items}} = 15.0\text{ sec}$; $0\text{ written words} \implies T_{\text{write}} = 0.0\text{ sec}$. **Block Total:** $90 + 60 + 15 + 0 = \mathbf{165.0\text{ sec}}$ ($2.75\text{ min} \le 210\text{ sec}$).
* **`Reinforce` Block:** $70\text{ printed words} \implies T_{\text{read}} = (70 / 70) \times 60 = 60.0\text{ sec}$; $1\text{ visual} \implies T_{\text{visual}} = 30.0\text{ sec}$; $3\text{ items} \implies T_{\text{items}} = 75.0\text{ sec}$; $4\text{ written words} \implies T_{\text{write}} = (4 / 11) \times 60 = 21.8\text{ sec}$. **Block Total:** $60 + 30 + 75 + 21.8 = \mathbf{186.8\text{ sec}}$ ($3.11\text{ min} \le 210\text{ sec}$).
* **`Vary` Block:** $56\text{ printed words} \implies T_{\text{read}} = (56 / 70) \times 60 = 48.0\text{ sec}$; $0\text{ visuals} \implies T_{\text{visual}} = 0.0\text{ sec}$; $2\text{ items} \implies T_{\text{items}} = 70.0\text{ sec}$; $6\text{ written words} \implies T_{\text{write}} = (6 / 11) \times 60 = 32.7\text{ sec}$. **Block Total:** $48 + 0 + 70 + 32.7 = \mathbf{150.7\text{ sec}}$ ($2.51\text{ min} \le 210\text{ sec}$).
* **`Apply` Block:** $70\text{ printed words} \implies T_{\text{read}} = (70 / 70) \times 60 = 60.0\text{ sec}$; $1\text{ visual} \implies T_{\text{visual}} = 30.0\text{ sec}$; $2\text{ items} \implies T_{\text{items}} = 110.0\text{ sec}$; $8\text{ written words} \implies T_{\text{write}} = (8 / 11) \times 60 = 43.6\text{ sec}$. **Block Total:** $60 + 30 + 110 + 43.6 = \mathbf{243.6\text{ sec}}$ ($4.06\text{ min} \le 270\text{ sec}$).
* **`Recall` Block:** $35\text{ printed words} \implies T_{\text{read}} = (35 / 70) \times 60 = 30.0\text{ sec}$; $0\text{ visuals} \implies T_{\text{visual}} = 0.0\text{ sec}$; $1\text{ item} \implies T_{\text{items}} = 45.0\text{ sec}$; $8\text{ written words} \implies T_{\text{write}} = (8 / 11) \times 60 = 43.6\text{ sec}$. **Block Total:** $30 + 0 + 45 + 43.6 = \mathbf{118.6\text{ sec}}$ ($1.98\text{ min} \le 210\text{ sec}$).
* **Lesson Totals:**
  * $T_{\text{stages}} = 165.0 + 186.8 + 150.7 + 243.6 + 118.6 = \mathbf{864.7\text{ sec}}$ ($14.41\text{ min}$).
  * $T_{\text{lesson}} = 60.0 + 864.7 = \mathbf{924.7\text{ sec}} = \mathbf{15.41\text{ min}} \in [780\text{ sec}, 990\text{ sec}]$ ($[13.0\text{ min}, 16.5\text{ min}]$).
  * `Apply + Recall` Share $= (243.6 + 118.6) / 864.7 = 362.2 / 864.7 = \mathbf{41.9\%} \in [35\%, 45\%]$.
  * Handwriting Volume $= 0 + 4 + 6 + 8 + 8 = \mathbf{26\text{ words}} \le 40\text{ words}$; Handwriting Time Share $= 141.7 / 864.7 = \mathbf{16.4\%} \le 30\%$.

### 6.3 Safe Timing & Overflow Adjustment Hierarchy (With Mandatory Re-Render & Full Revalidation)
When a draft lesson falls outside $[780\text{ sec}, 990\text{ sec}]$ ($[13.0, 16.5]\text{ min}$), violates the `Apply + Recall` balance, or overflows the 2-page student canvas, the software **must never blindly delete random items in code**. Instead, it executes a targeted adjustment pass following the pedagogical priority order below.

**Critical Revalidation Rule:** Every adjustment or repair produces a modified lesson draft that **must undergo a fresh headless HTML/CSS render and complete revalidation across all `DET-01`–`DET-08` and `AI-01`–`AI-06` checks**. An attempted adjustment is never treated as proof of compliance; if the re-rendered lesson still fails any check after the maximum 2-retry limit, generation stops and outputs a structured Failure Report.

| Condition | Safe Pedagogical Adjustment Hierarchy (Followed by Mandatory Re-Render & Full Revalidation) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Case 1: Lesson Too Long ($T_{\text{lesson}} > 990\text{ sec}$) or Overflows 2 Pages** | **Step 1 (Tighten Text & Motor Load First):** Condense instructional prose and story context by $20\text{–}30\%$, and convert high-handwriting prompts (`M9`) in `Reinforce`/`Vary` into low-motor mechanics (`M2` matching or `M4` letter-code sorting) while keeping all concepts and stages intact.<br>**Step 2 (Consolidate Redundant Sub-Items Only):** If still $>990\text{ sec}$ on revalidation, reduce a 4-item block in `Reinforce` or `Vary` to 2–3 items *only* when multiple items test the exact same sub-concept—never dropping any stage below its minimum item count (`Section 4.1`) or removing test coverage of a taught sub-concept.<br>**Step 3 (Narrow Curriculum Scope):** If the lesson still exceeds $990\text{ sec}$ at minimum item counts, narrow the curriculum slice from 2–3 sub-concepts down to 1 foundational sub-concept. |
| **Case 2: Lesson Too Short ($T_{\text{lesson}} < 780\text{ sec}$)** | **Step 1 (Deepen Practice & Application):** Add 1 varied practice item to `Vary` (up to 3 items) or 1 problem scenario to `Apply` (up to 2 items) testing a different angle or boundary condition of the taught concept.<br>**Step 2 (Upgrade Cognitive Engagement):** If item counts are already near the upper bound, upgrade a passive recognition item in `Reinforce` or `Vary` into a faded-step completion (`M5`), diagram labelling (`M3`), or error-spotting (`M6`) task. Never pad time by adding filler prose or decorative games. |
| **Case 3: `Apply + Recall` Share Outside $35\%\text{–}45\%$** | **If $< 35\%$:** Trim 1 redundant item or excess prose from `Discover`/`Reinforce` and add 1 structured step or synthesis prompt to `Apply`.<br>**If $> 45\%$:** Convert an overly long written response in `Apply` to a structured table/diagram completion (`M3`/`M8`) or add 1 scaffolded practice item to `Reinforce`. |

## 8. Version 1.2 Technical Verification Architecture

**Foundational Principle:** Automated checks (both deterministic code and AI reviewers) are **defect filters, not guarantees of educational quality**. Version 1.2 separates verification into **Category A (Deterministic Software Checks)**, **Category B (Contextual AI-Assisted Checks)**, and **Category C (Human Validation)**.

### 8.1 Category A: Deterministic Software Checks (`DET-01` to `DET-08`)

### 8.3 Mandatory Re-Render, Complete Revalidation & Bounded Retry Policy (`[Mandatory V1 Product Constraint]`)
To ensure repairs never introduce new regressions (such as a timing fix causing a layout overflow, or an instruction edit breaking answerability):
1. **Fresh Render & Complete Revalidation After Every Repair:** Whenever any `DET` or `AI` check fails and triggers a targeted repair (including any Section 6.3 timing or layout adjustment), the modified lesson JSON **must be freshly compiled and rendered into the headless HTML/CSS DOM** and **must re-run the complete verification suite (`DET-01` through `DET-08` and `AI-01` through `AI-06`) from the start**.
2. **Never Treat an Attempted Repair as Proof of Success:** Applying a repair heuristic does not mark a check as passed; the repaired artifact passes *only* if the subsequent fresh render and full revalidation pass 100% of blocking gates.
3. **Bounded Retry Limit (Maximum 2 Retries) & Failure Report:** A lesson draft is permitted at most **2 repair-and-revalidation cycles**. If any blocking `DET` or `AI` check still fails after the second revalidation, the software **immediately stops and blocks delivery**, emitting a structured **Generation Failure Report** that logs the failed check ID(s), the specific block/item that failed revalidation, and the required user or operator action.

### 8.4 Category C: Stateless Human Validation Protocol (`HUM-01` to `HUM-04`)
Because automated checks cannot verify real child comprehension, attention, or physical paper usability, Version 1.2 mandates four practical human validation workflows that operate **without** student profiles, uploaded student work, or adaptive follow-up lessons:
